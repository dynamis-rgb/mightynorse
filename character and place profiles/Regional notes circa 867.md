# Regional Gazetteer, c. 867

A reference guide to the political, demographic, linguistic, religious, and infrastructural landscape of the western Baltic, Saxony, Thuringia, Sorbia, Franconia, and Denmark around the year 867.

> **Demographic caution:** Exact population figures are generally unavailable for these regions in the ninth century. Population density and settlement scale are therefore best treated comparatively rather than as precise census data.

---

## 1. Wagria / Eastern Holstein

**Political identity:** Wagrian, within the broader Obotrite federation.

**Approximate area:** Baltic-facing eastern Holstein, centered especially on Starigard/Oldenburg.

**Population:** Sparse to moderately settled rural landscape, with population clustered around favorable agricultural land, waterways, and fortified centers.

**Predominant religion:** Traditional West Slavic polytheism.

**Minority religion:** Christianity, probably limited mainly to elites, diplomats, captives, merchants, and people exposed to Frankish missionary activity.

**Predominant language:** Local West Slavic/Wagrian speech.

**Minority/contact languages:** Old Saxon near the western frontier; Old Norse/Danish in maritime and trading environments; some Latin among clergy and diplomats.

**Rural infrastructure:** Wooden houses, agricultural clearings, wells, barns, pens, earth tracks, ferries, and water routes.

**Urban/central-place infrastructure:** Strongholds with earth-and-timber ramparts, elite halls, workshops, storehouses, and nearby dependent settlements.

**Major center:** **Starigard** (modern Oldenburg in Holstein), a significant Wagrian stronghold and elite center.

**Liubice:** Probably still a relatively modest settlement in 867; its major political importance belongs primarily to the eleventh century.

---

## 2. Polabia Proper / Lower-Elbe Slavic Country

**Political identity:** Polabians proper, one component of the broader Obotrite federation.

**Approximate area:** Broadly between the Trave and lower Elbe, south and southwest of Wagria.

**Population:** Sparse to moderate, concentrated in agricultural settlement clusters separated by woodland and wetlands.

**Predominant religion:** Traditional Slavic religion.

**Minority religion:** Christianity among a small number of elites or people tied to Frankish networks.

**Predominant language:** West Slavic, closely related to Wagrian and Obotrite speech.

**Minority/contact languages:** Old Saxon in border and trade environments; some Norse in commercial contexts.

**Infrastructure:** Villages, fortified centers, river crossings, ferries, forest tracks, and elite residences.

**General character:** The transition from Wagria into Polabia would likely feel culturally gradual rather than abrupt.

---

## 3. Obotrites Proper / Western Mecklenburg

**Political identity:** Core Obotrite territory and political heartland of the larger federation.

**Approximate area:** Broadly the Wismar–Schwerin region.

**Population:** Sparse to moderately settled rural country organized around fortified settlement districts.

**Predominant religion:** Traditional Slavic religion.

**Minority religion:** Christianity among some elites and individuals connected to Carolingian diplomacy.

**Predominant language:** West Slavic, closely related to Wagrian and Polabian varieties.

**Minority/contact languages:** Old Saxon and Norse in political and commercial settings; limited Latin among clergy or diplomatic personnel.

**Infrastructure:** Stronghold-settlement complexes, elite residences, workshops, agricultural villages, river and lake travel routes.

**Major centers and sites:**
- **Mecklenburg**
- **Ilow**
- **Friedrichsruhe**
- Former emporium of **Reric**, destroyed in 808

**General character:** Political authority is expressed through strongholds and elite households rather than urban institutions.

---

## 4. Warnower / Eastern Mecklenburg

**Political identity:** Warnower and related eastern Obotrite-associated groups.

**Approximate area:** Around the Warnow and eastern Mecklenburg.

**Population:** Sparse, organized around local strongholds and village clusters.

**Predominant religion:** Traditional Slavic religion.

**Minority religion:** Very limited Christianity.

**Predominant language:** West Slavic.

**Minority/contact languages:** Other Slavic varieties; some Norse and Saxon through trade or political contact.

**Infrastructure:** Fortified centers, villages, craft sites, waterways, forest routes.

**Notable site:** **Groß Raden**, important archaeologically for its ninth- to tenth-century Slavic settlement and religious structures.

**General character:** More visibly Slavic and less directly influenced by Frankish institutions than the western frontier.

---

## 5. Wilzian / Veletian Lands

**Political identity:** Wilzi/Veleti, a major Polabian Slavic confederation distinct from the Obotrites.

**Approximate area:** East and southeast of the Obotrite federation.

**Population:** Sparse agricultural landscape with stronghold-centered settlement.

**Predominant religion:** Traditional Slavic religion.

**Minority religion:** Very limited Christianity.

**Predominant language:** West Slavic.

**Minority/contact languages:** Related Slavic varieties, with some Saxon or Frankish contact in diplomacy and warfare.

**Infrastructure:** Strongholds, rural villages, river routes, woodland tracks.

**Political structure:** Decentralized federation of regional tribes and stronghold districts.

**General character:** Culturally related to the Obotrites but politically distinct and often rivalrous.

---

## 6. Nordalbingian Saxony / Holstein and Stormarn

**Political identity:** Saxon, within East Francia.

**Approximate area:** Western and central Holstein, Stormarn, and the lower-Elbe Saxon zone.

**Population:** Moderate rural density compared with much of Mecklenburg, though still overwhelmingly rural.

**Predominant religion:** Latin Christianity.

**Minority religion:** Surviving pre-Christian customs, beliefs, and folk practices.

**Predominant language:** Old Saxon.

**Minority/contact languages:** Slavic near the eastern frontier; Norse/Danish near trade corridors; Latin among clergy and officials.

**Rural infrastructure:** Farmsteads, villages, roads and tracks, bridges, ferries, parish or mission churches, estate centers.

**Military infrastructure:** Frankish and Saxon strongpoints controlling routes and frontier corridors.

**Important centers and fortifications:**
- **Hamburg/Hammaburg**
- **Esesfelth**
- Other fortified frontier nodes

**General character:** More fully integrated into Carolingian ecclesiastical and military structures than Wagria, but still a recently conquered frontier society.

---

## 7. Eastphalian / Central Saxony

**Political identity:** Saxon, within East Francia.

**Approximate area:** Bremen, Hildesheim, Paderborn, Corvey, and surrounding districts.

**Population:** Moderate rural density, with stronger institutional concentration around episcopal and monastic centers.

**Predominant religion:** Latin Christianity.

**Minority religion:** Residual folk practices and pre-Christian traditions.

**Predominant language:** Old Saxon.

**Minority/contact languages:** Latin among clergy and educated elites; some Norse, Slavic, and Frisian among travelers, merchants, and missionaries.

**Rural infrastructure:** Estate villages, church lands, mills, ferries, bridges, roads, agricultural estates.

**Urban/central-place infrastructure:** Cathedrals, monasteries, royal or aristocratic residences, markets, storehouses, workshops, scribal administration.

**Major centers:**
- **Bremen**
- **Hildesheim**
- **Corvey**
- **Paderborn**

**General character:** Noticeably more institutional, literate, and administratively organized than the Slavic lands to the east.

---

## 8. Thuringia

**Political identity:** Thuringian, within East Francia.

**Approximate area:** Central Germany between Saxony, Franconia, and the Sorbian frontier.

**Population:** Moderate, with denser settlement in fertile valleys and basins and sparser occupation in wooded uplands.

**Predominant religion:** Latin Christianity.

**Minority religion:** Folk beliefs and residual pre-Christian practices.

**Predominant language:** Old High German central dialects, including Thuringian varieties.

**Minority/contact languages:** Old Saxon toward the north; Sorbian Slavic near the Saale frontier; Latin among clergy and officials.

**Rural infrastructure:** Villages, estate centers, roads, ferries, bridges, mills, monastic properties.

**Frontier infrastructure:** Fortified crossings, military musters, toll points, and administrative sites near the Saale.

**Major center:** **Erfurt**

**Other important nodes:** Hersfeld and routes toward Fulda.

**General character:** A developed East Frankish rural region whose eastern edge functions as active frontier country.

---

## 9. Sorbian Lands

**Political identity:** Sorbian and related regional Slavic groups, not a single centralized kingdom.

**Approximate area:** East of the Saale and across the middle Elbe–Saale frontier.

**Population:** Sparse to moderate, concentrated around villages and fortified centers.

**Predominant religion:** Traditional Slavic religion.

**Minority religion:** Limited Christianity, especially near the frontier and among elites exposed to East Frankish influence.

**Predominant language:** West Slavic Sorbian varieties.

**Minority/contact languages:** Thuringian and other Germanic dialects near the frontier; some Latin among diplomatic intermediaries.

**Infrastructure:** Strongholds, rural villages, forest tracks, river routes, frontier crossings.

**General character:** Inland frontier society subject to recurring tribute, raids, hostages, military expeditions, and political negotiation with East Francia.

---

## 10. Hesse / Saxon–Thuringian–Frankish Borderlands

**Political identity:** East Frankish, with mixed regional ties to Saxony, Thuringia, and Franconia.

**Population:** Moderate, with scattered royal and monastic estates and large forest zones.

**Predominant religion:** Latin Christianity.

**Predominant language:** Old High German dialects, with Old Saxon influence in the north.

**Minority/contact languages:** Latin among clergy and educated elites.

**Infrastructure:** Monasteries, royal estates, roads, ferries, bridges, agricultural settlements.

**Major centers:**
- **Fulda**
- **Hersfeld**
- **Fritzlar**

**General character:** Transitional zone connecting the Saxon, Thuringian, and Franconian worlds.

---

## 11. Franconia Proper

**Political identity:** Frankish, within East Francia.

**Approximate area:** Main valley and adjacent central German regions, but not extending west to the Lotharingian border for this gazetteer.

**Population:** Comparatively high by regional standards, especially in river valleys and around major ecclesiastical and royal centers.

**Predominant religion:** Latin Christianity.

**Minority religion:** Residual folk practices; Jewish presence possible in major commercial centers but should be established locally rather than assumed universally.

**Predominant language:** Old High German, especially East Frankish dialects.

**Minority/contact languages:** Latin among clergy, officials, and educated elites; other Germanic dialects among travelers.

**Rural infrastructure:** Dense estate networks, villages, mills, roads, ferries, bridges, vineyards where appropriate, church estates.

**Urban/central-place infrastructure:** Cathedral settlements, monasteries, royal palaces, markets, workshops, written administration.

**Major centers:**
- **Fulda**
- **Würzburg**
- **Frankfurt**
- **Mainz** at the western edge of this broader zone

**General character:** The most administratively and ecclesiastically developed region in this survey.

---

## 12. Denmark

**Political identity:** Danish kingdoms and regional lordships.

**Population:** Overwhelmingly rural. Broad archaeological estimates suggest roughly several hundred thousand inhabitants in the territory corresponding approximately to modern Denmark around the ninth century.

**Predominant religion:** Norse paganism.

**Minority religion:** Christianity, especially around missionary activity and elite or trading settlements.

**Predominant language:** Old Norse / early Danish dialect continuum.

**Minority/contact languages:** Old Saxon, Slavic languages, Frisian, and some Latin among clergy.

**Rural infrastructure:** Farms, halls, small villages, coastal landing places, tracks, ferries, and strong reliance on maritime transport.

**Urban/central-place infrastructure:** Rare but highly significant emporia.

**Major centers:**
- **Hedeby**
- **Ribe**

**General character:** Rural overall, but with a few extraordinarily cosmopolitan commercial centers.

# Relative Population Density

Because reliable ninth-century census figures do not exist for most of these regions, the following is best understood comparatively.

## Very sparse
- Wilzian interior
- Much of Sorbia
- Wooded Mecklenburg interiors

## Sparse but organized into settlement clusters
- Obotrite core
- Warnower country
- Polabia
- Wagria

## Moderate rural density
- Nordalbingian Saxony
- Interior Denmark
- Eastern Thuringia

## Moderate to comparatively high
- Central Saxony
- Central Thuringia
- Hesse

## Highest within this survey
- Main valley and Franconian districts around Frankfurt, Würzburg, and Mainz

Even the densest regions remain overwhelmingly rural.

# Settlement Types Around 867

## Ordinary Rural Settlement
A few extended families to a few hundred people.

Typical features:
- Wooden houses
- Barns and pens
- Wells
- Hearths
- Small agricultural clearings
- Dirt tracks

## Elite Estate or Hall
Residence of a local noble or major landholder.

Typical features:
- Great hall or elite residence
- Dependents and servants
- Storage buildings
- Workshops
- Agricultural land
- Armed retainers

## Stronghold-Settlement Complex
Especially common and politically important in Slavic regions.

Typical features:
- Earth-and-timber ramparts
- Elite residence
- Craft production
- Storage
- Nearby villages
- Defensive position near waterways or routes

Examples:
- Starigard
- Mecklenburg
- Friedrichsruhe

## Ecclesiastical or Royal Center
More characteristic of East Francia.

Typical features:
- Cathedral or monastery
- Clergy
- Aristocratic residences
- Workshops
- Markets
- Servants
- Written administration
- Royal or episcopal officials

Examples:
- Hildesheim
- Bremen
- Paderborn
- Fulda
- Frankfurt

## Emporium
Rare international commercial settlement.

Typical features:
- Merchants from multiple regions
- Warehouses
- Craft production
- Imported goods
- Multilingual population
- Strong political protection

Best example:
- **Hedeby**

# Westward Cultural Progression for Travelers

A traveler moving west from the Liubice region might experience the landscape roughly like this:

**Liubice**  
Small Slavic settlement landscape

↓

**Wagria / Starigard**  
Organized Slavic principality with strongholds and elite halls

↓

**Saxon frontier**  
Different language, Christian institutions, fortified crossings

↓

**Bremen / Hildesheim**  
Bishops, scribes, markets, larger institutions, much more Latin writing

↓

**Thuringia**  
Denser agricultural country, East Frankish aristocratic estates, frontier military organization

↓

**Franconia**  
Monasteries, royal estates, major churches, markets, and stronger administrative networks

A separate journey to **Hedeby** would reveal a different kind of sophistication: commercially cosmopolitan, multilingual, and highly connected despite being predominantly Norse pagan.

# Recommended Regional Set

For a story centered on travel between the western Baltic and East Francia around 867, the most useful regional categories are:

1. Wagria
2. Polabia Proper
3. Obotrites Proper / Western Mecklenburg
4. Warnower / Eastern Mecklenburg
5. Wilzi / Veleti
6. Nordalbingian Saxony
7. Eastphalian / Central Saxony
8. Thuringia
9. Sorbia
10. Hesse
11. Franconia
12. Denmark

Possible later additions:
- Bohemia
- Moravia
- Frisia
- Lotharingia
